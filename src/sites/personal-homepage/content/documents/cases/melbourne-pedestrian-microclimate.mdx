<ArchiveSection id="questions" title="三个问题，一条开放数据链。" eyebrow="01 / 问题">

这个项目问三件事：墨尔本 CBD 的行人活动随时间怎么变化？温度、风和颗粒物条件与活动之间有什么关联？哪些地段对高温和差空气质量更脆弱，工作日和周末又是否不同？

原料全部来自墨尔本市政府的官方开放数据：147 万行行人计数、145 行传感器位置、59 万行微气候观测。三个数据源各有各的发布频率和坐标习惯——这个项目真正的起点不是建模，而是把口径对齐。

</ArchiveSection>

<ArchiveSection id="pipeline" title="先可复现，再可解释。" eyebrow="02 / 管线">

四段式 R 管线（下载 → 清洗 → 成图 → 报告）覆盖全部处理过程：`data.table` 做过滤与连接，`lubridate` 处理时区并聚合到小时，自写的半正矢距离计算完成"每个行人传感器匹配最近微气候设备"的空间连接。150 米的严格阈值保留了 14 个传感器，中位匹配距离 99.7 米。

原始数据收敛为 170,243 行联表小时数据，其中 130,918 行具备完整的温度、PM2.5 与风速。缺失值不插补——这是探索性分析，不是预测比赛，把"哪里有洞"留在明处比把洞填上更重要。

</ArchiveSection>

<ArchiveSection id="findings" title="环境信号存在，但长在城市节奏之上。" eyebrow="03 / 发现">

三个问题都有可核验的答案。活动按月、按星期、按小时的结构性差异显著，且不同位置的性格完全不同——通勤型地段和休闲型地段的高峰形状不一样。控制这些节奏之后，温度、PM2.5 与风仍与活动存在可测的关联。

分传感器的高温与 PM2.5 敏感度地图显示：脆弱性不是单维度的，同一地段对高温和 对污染的响应可以不一致，工作日与周末的形态也会分开。这项分析是观察性的，它给出的是条件关联和空间格局，不是因果效应——限制写在报告里，也写在每一次重跑里。

</ArchiveSection>

<ArchiveSection id="questions" title="Three questions, one open-data chain." eyebrow="01 / Questions">

The project asks three things: how does pedestrian activity across the Melbourne CBD vary over time? How are temperature, wind, and particulate conditions associated with it? And which locations look more vulnerable to heat and poor air quality — and do weekdays and weekends disagree?

The raw material comes entirely from the City of Melbourne's official open data: 1.47 million pedestrian count records, 145 sensor-location rows, and 591 thousand microclimate observations. Each source ships with its own cadence and coordinate habits — the real starting point of this project was not modelling but aligning definitions.

</ArchiveSection>

<ArchiveSection id="pipeline" title="Reproducible first, interpretable second." eyebrow="02 / Pipeline">

A four-stage R pipeline (download → clean → figures → report) carries the whole process: `data.table` for filtering and joins, `lubridate` for time zones and hourly aggregation, and a hand-written haversine calculation for the spatial join that matches each pedestrian sensor to its nearest microclimate device. The strict 150-metre threshold keeps 14 sensors at a median match distance of 99.7 metres.

The raw data converge into 170,243 joined hourly rows, 130,918 of them with complete temperature, PM2.5, and wind. Missing readings are not imputed — this is exploratory analysis, not a competition, and leaving the holes visible matters more than filling them.

</ArchiveSection>

<ArchiveSection id="findings" title="The environmental signal exists, but it grows on top of the urban rhythm." eyebrow="03 / Findings">

All three questions get checkable answers. Activity varies strongly by month, weekday, and hour, and locations have distinct personalities — commuter districts and leisure districts peak in different shapes. Once those rhythms are controlled, temperature, PM2.5, and wind still show measurable associations with activity.

Per-sensor heat and PM2.5 sensitivity maps show that vulnerability is not one-dimensional: the same location can respond differently to heat than to pollution, and weekday patterns split from weekend ones. The analysis is observational — it offers conditional associations and spatial patterns, not causal effects. The limits live in the report, and in every rerun.

</ArchiveSection>
